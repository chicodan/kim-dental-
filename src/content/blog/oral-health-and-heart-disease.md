When you think about protecting your heart, brushing and flossing probably aren't the first things that come to mind. But research over the past two decades has revealed a fascinating connection between what's happening in your mouth and the health of your cardiovascular system. Understanding this link could be one of the most important steps you take for your overall wellbeing.

## How Your Mouth Affects Your Heart

The connection between oral health and heart disease centers largely on **inflammation and bacteria**. When gum disease develops, harmful bacteria can enter your bloodstream through inflamed gum tissue. These bacteria don't just stay in your mouth—they travel throughout your body, potentially reaching your heart and blood vessels.

Studies have shown that people with **periodontal (gum) disease** are nearly twice as likely to have coronary artery disease compared to those with healthy gums. The bacteria associated with gum disease have actually been found in arterial plaque, the fatty deposits that can clog arteries and lead to heart attacks and strokes.

Beyond bacteria, the chronic inflammation caused by gum disease may contribute to **atherosclerosis**, the hardening and narrowing of arteries. When your body is constantly fighting infection in your gums, it triggers an inflammatory response that can affect blood vessel walls throughout your entire system.

## Warning Signs to Watch For

Many people don't realize they have gum disease because the early stages are often painless. Here are some signs that shouldn't be ignored:

- **Gums that bleed** when you brush or floss
- Red, swollen, or tender gum tissue
- Persistent bad breath that doesn't improve with brushing
- Gums that appear to be **pulling away from teeth**
- Loose teeth or changes in how your bite feels

If you notice any of these symptoms, it's worth scheduling a dental visit sooner rather than later. Early intervention can prevent gum disease from progressing and potentially reduce your cardiovascular risk.

## Simple Steps to Protect Both Your Smile and Your Heart

The good news is that maintaining good oral health doesn't require complicated routines. Here's what makes the biggest difference:

**Brush twice daily** for at least two minutes each time, using a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste. Many of our patients here in Paramus, NJ have found that electric toothbrushes make it easier to reach recommended brushing time.

**Floss every day**—yes, every day. This removes plaque and food particles from between teeth where your toothbrush can't reach. If traditional floss is difficult for you, water flossers are an excellent alternative.

**Don't skip your regular dental checkups**. Professional cleanings remove hardened tartar that home care can't address, and your dentist can catch early signs of gum disease before they become serious problems.

Here's a practical tip you can start today: **add an antimicrobial mouthwash** to your routine. Rinsing for 30 seconds after brushing can help reduce the bacteria that contribute to gum disease. Look for products with the ADA Seal of Acceptance.

## The Bigger Picture

Your mouth truly is a window to your overall health. When patients take care of their gums, they're not just preserving their smile—they're taking meaningful action to support their heart health and reduce systemic inflammation throughout their body.

If you have existing heart conditions, diabetes, or other health concerns, maintaining excellent oral health becomes even more important. Be sure to keep your dentist informed about any medications you take or changes to your medical history, as this helps us provide the safest, most effective care.
