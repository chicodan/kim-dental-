Have you ever noticed that your teeth look a bit longer than they used to, or felt some sensitivity when drinking cold water? You might be experiencing gum recession, a common dental condition that affects millions of Americans. The good news is that gum recession is often preventable and treatable when caught early. Let's explore what causes your gums to pull back and what you can do to protect your smile.

## What Is Gum Recession?

**Gum recession** occurs when the gum tissue surrounding your teeth wears away or pulls back, exposing more of the tooth or even the tooth's root. This gradual process can happen so slowly that many people don't notice it until they experience symptoms like increased tooth sensitivity or visible changes in their smile.

When gums recede, small gaps or "pockets" can form between the teeth and gum line, making it easier for harmful bacteria to accumulate. Left untreated, this can lead to **damage to the supporting bone and tissue**, potentially resulting in tooth loss. That's why understanding the causes and taking preventive action matters so much for your long-term oral health.

## Common Causes of Gum Recession

Several factors can contribute to receding gums, and sometimes it's a combination of issues working together:

**Aggressive brushing** is one of the most common culprits. Many people think brushing harder means cleaner teeth, but using too much pressure or a hard-bristled toothbrush can actually wear away your enamel and damage delicate gum tissue over time.

**Periodontal disease** (gum disease) is the leading cause of gum recession. When bacteria build up along the gum line and aren't properly removed, they can cause inflammation and infection that destroys gum tissue and the bone supporting your teeth.

**Genetics** also play a role. Some people are simply more susceptible to gum recession regardless of how well they care for their teeth. If your parents experienced receding gums, you may want to be extra vigilant about your gum health.

Other contributing factors include **teeth grinding or clenching**, tobacco use, hormonal changes (particularly in women), misaligned teeth, and even lip or tongue piercings that can irritate and wear away gum tissue.

## What You Can Do About It

The best approach to gum recession depends on how severe it is. For mild cases, your dentist may recommend **non-surgical treatments** like professional deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) to remove plaque and tartar buildup below the gum line. This allows the gums to heal and reattach to the teeth.

For more advanced recession, **gum grafting surgery** may be necessary. This procedure involves taking tissue from another part of your mouth (often the roof) and attaching it to the affected area to cover exposed roots and prevent further recession.

Here in Bergen County, we see patients regularly who are concerned about changes in their gum line. The key is addressing the issue early before it progresses.

**Here's a practical tip you can start using today:** Switch to a soft-bristled toothbrush and practice gentle, circular brushing motions rather than aggressive back-and-forth scrubbing. Hold your toothbrush at a 45-degree angle to your gums and let the bristles do the work. This simple change can make a significant difference in protecting your gum tissue.

Additionally, make sure you're flossing daily, avoiding tobacco products, and keeping up with your regular dental checkups. If you grind your teeth at night, ask your dentist about a custom nightguard to protect both your teeth and gums.
