Most of us learned to brush our teeth as kids and haven't thought much about our technique since. But here's something that might surprise you: many adults are still brushing their teeth incorrectly, even after decades of daily practice. The good news? A few simple adjustments can make a significant difference in your oral health, helping you prevent cavities, gum disease, and unnecessary trips to the dentist.

## The Most Common Brushing Mistakes

**Brushing too hard** is one of the most frequent errors we see at our practice. Many patients believe that applying more pressure means cleaner teeth, but this aggressive approach can actually damage your enamel and irritate your gums. Over time, hard brushing can lead to gum recession and tooth sensitivity—problems that are much harder to fix than to prevent.

Another common mistake is **brushing too quickly**. The American Dental Association recommends brushing for a full two minutes, twice daily. Most people, however, spend less than a minute on the task. Think about it: when did you last actually time yourself? You might be surprised at how short your routine really is.

Using a **back-and-forth sawing motion** is also problematic. While it feels productive, this technique misses the areas where plaque tends to accumulate most—along the gumline and between teeth.

## The Proper Brushing Technique

The correct method starts with **holding your toothbrush at a 45-degree angle** to your gums. This positioning allows the bristles to reach just beneath the gumline, where bacteria love to hide.

Instead of scrubbing horizontally, use **gentle, circular motions** or short back-and-forth strokes about a tooth-wide. Work methodically around your mouth, spending about 30 seconds on each quadrant: upper right, upper left, lower right, and lower left.

Don't forget to **brush all surfaces** of your teeth—the outer surfaces facing your cheeks, the inner surfaces facing your tongue, and the chewing surfaces on top. Many people neglect the inner surfaces, especially on the back molars, where decay frequently develops.

**Brush your tongue** as well, using gentle strokes from back to front. Your tongue harbors bacteria that contribute to bad breath and can redeposit on your teeth after brushing.

## Choosing the Right Tools

Your technique matters, but so does your equipment. Dentists generally recommend using a **soft-bristled toothbrush** because it effectively removes plaque without damaging your gums or enamel. Medium and hard bristles are simply too abrasive for most people.

Consider switching to an **electric toothbrush** if you struggle with proper technique. Many models have built-in timers and pressure sensors that help you brush for the right amount of time without pressing too hard. Studies consistently show that electric toothbrushes remove more plaque than manual brushing.

**Replace your toothbrush every three to four months**, or sooner if the bristles look frayed. Worn bristles don't clean effectively and can harbor bacteria.

Here's a practical tip you can try tonight: set a timer on your phone for two minutes and focus on using gentle pressure with circular motions. Many patients in Bergen County tell us they're amazed at how different—and how much more thorough—their brushing feels when they slow down and pay attention.

## Beyond Brushing

Remember that even perfect brushing technique can't do everything. **Daily flossing** is essential for cleaning between teeth where your brush can't reach. And regular professional cleanings allow us to remove tartar buildup and catch potential problems early, before they become painful or expensive to treat.
