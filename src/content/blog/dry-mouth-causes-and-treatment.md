Have you ever woken up with a parched feeling in your mouth, or noticed that your throat feels scratchy and dry throughout the day? While occasional dry mouth is normal, persistent dryness—known as **xerostomia**—is more than just uncomfortable. It can actually put your dental health at serious risk. Understanding why dry mouth happens and what you can do about it is an important step in protecting your smile.

## What Causes Dry Mouth?

**Saliva** does so much more than we often realize. It helps us chew and swallow food, neutralizes harmful acids, and washes away food particles and bacteria. When your salivary glands don't produce enough saliva, that's when dry mouth becomes a problem.

There are several common reasons why you might experience xerostomia:

- **Medications** are one of the most frequent culprits. Hundreds of prescription and over-the-counter drugs—including antihistamines, blood pressure medications, antidepressants, and pain relievers—list dry mouth as a side effect.
- **Medical conditions** like diabetes, Sjögren's syndrome, and autoimmune disorders can affect saliva production.
- **Cancer treatments** such as radiation therapy to the head and neck area can damage salivary glands, sometimes permanently.
- **Lifestyle factors** including mouth breathing, smoking, and alcohol consumption can contribute to chronic dryness.
- **Natural aging** also plays a role, though dry mouth isn't an inevitable part of getting older.

## Why Dry Mouth Matters for Your Teeth

Here's something that might surprise you: **saliva is your mouth's natural defense system**. Without adequate saliva flow, your teeth become much more vulnerable to decay. In fact, patients with chronic dry mouth often experience a dramatic increase in cavities, even if they've never had many before.

When your mouth stays dry, bacteria thrive. Acids from food and bacteria linger on your teeth longer, wearing away protective enamel. You might also notice **bad breath**, difficulty speaking or swallowing, and even changes in how food tastes. Some people develop sores or a burning sensation in their mouth.

For our patients here in Paramus and throughout Bergen County, we've seen firsthand how untreated xerostomia can lead to rapid dental deterioration. What starts as mild discomfort can quickly progress to serious oral health issues if left unaddressed.

## Practical Tips to Manage Dry Mouth

The good news is that there are several things you can do right now to help relieve dry mouth symptoms and protect your teeth:

**Stay hydrated throughout the day.** Sipping water regularly—especially during meals—can help compensate for reduced saliva. Keep a water bottle with you and take small sips often rather than large amounts at once.

Other helpful strategies include:

- Chewing **sugar-free gum** or sucking on sugar-free candies to stimulate saliva production
- Using a **humidifier** in your bedroom at night
- Avoiding alcohol-based mouthwashes, which can worsen dryness
- Limiting caffeine and alcohol intake
- Breathing through your nose whenever possible

If medications are causing your dry mouth, **don't stop taking them without consulting your doctor**. Instead, ask about alternatives or timing adjustments that might help. There are also prescription saliva substitutes and medications that can stimulate saliva production for more severe cases.

From a dental perspective, patients with xerostomia often benefit from **prescription-strength fluoride toothpaste** and more frequent professional cleanings to stay ahead of potential decay.
