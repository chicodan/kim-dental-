Pregnancy is an exciting journey filled with changes—some expected, and others that might catch you by surprise. One area that often gets overlooked during this busy time is dental health. Many expecting mothers don't realize that pregnancy can significantly affect their oral health, and that taking care of their teeth and gums is an important part of prenatal care.

At Kim Dental, we regularly see pregnant patients who have questions about what's safe during pregnancy and what changes they might experience. Let's walk through everything you need to know to keep your smile healthy while you're expecting.

## How Pregnancy Affects Your Oral Health

Those pregnancy hormones that cause so many changes throughout your body also affect your mouth. **Elevated progesterone and estrogen levels** can make your gums more sensitive to plaque, leading to a condition called **pregnancy gingivitis**. You might notice your gums look redder than usual, feel tender, or bleed when you brush or floss.

This is incredibly common—affecting up to 75% of pregnant women—and usually appears sometime during the second trimester. While it typically resolves after delivery, it shouldn't be ignored. Untreated gingivitis can progress to more serious gum disease, which some studies have linked to preterm birth and low birth weight.

Morning sickness presents another challenge. If you're experiencing frequent vomiting, the stomach acid can erode your tooth enamel over time. **Rather than brushing immediately after getting sick**, rinse your mouth with water or a mixture of water and baking soda. Wait about 30 minutes before brushing to avoid spreading the acid across your teeth while the enamel is temporarily softened.

## Is Dental Care Safe During Pregnancy?

Here's some good news: **routine dental care is not only safe during pregnancy—it's recommended**. The American Dental Association and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists both encourage pregnant women to continue regular dental visits.

Dental cleanings, cavity fillings, and even X-rays (with proper shielding) can be performed safely throughout pregnancy. The second trimester is often the most comfortable time for dental work, but necessary treatment shouldn't be postponed regardless of which trimester you're in.

If you need dental work, be sure to let us know you're pregnant and how far along you are. We'll take extra precautions and can coordinate with your OB-GYN if needed. Local anesthetics like lidocaine are considered safe, and we'll always use the lowest effective dose.

## Practical Tips for a Healthy Smile During Pregnancy

Taking care of your teeth during pregnancy doesn't require anything complicated—just consistent attention to the basics:

**Brush twice daily** with fluoride toothpaste and **floss once a day**. If your gums are more sensitive than usual, try a soft-bristled brush and be gentle but thorough.

**Choose tooth-friendly snacks** when pregnancy cravings hit. Fresh fruits, vegetables, cheese, and yogurt are better choices than sugary treats that feed cavity-causing bacteria.

**Stay hydrated** throughout the day. Drinking plenty of water helps wash away food particles and keeps saliva flowing, which naturally protects your teeth.

**Don't skip dental appointments** just because you're pregnant. Many expecting mothers here in Bergen County tell us they assumed they should wait until after delivery, but maintaining your regular cleaning schedule is one of the best things you can do for your oral health during this time.

If you're experiencing severe morning sickness and struggling to brush without triggering your gag reflex, try switching to a bland-tasting toothpaste or brushing at different times of day when nausea is less intense.
